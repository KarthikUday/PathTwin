# PathTwin: Decisions and Limitations

This document summarizes the principal methodological decisions, data-verification findings, and limitations underlying the PathTwin pipeline. It allows an independent reviewer to evaluate the reasoning behind key choices without reconstructing them from version-control history. Quantitative results are reported in full in `README.md` and in the corresponding files under `results/`; this document provides the reasoning and context behind those results, organized by pipeline stage.

## Stage 2: Molecular Docking Validation

### Docking Accuracy Validation

Pose-reproduction accuracy, whether a docked ligand reproduces its known crystallographic position, is a distinct question from screening accuracy: whether the method actually ranks a true ligand above property-matched inactive molecules. No existing benchmark dataset provided matched decoys for this project's target classes, so a decoy set was generated independently for each of the eight targets, matched to the true ligand's molecular weight and lipophilicity, required to be topologically dissimilar, and drawn only from real, resolvable compound records.

Redocking each target's true ligand against its generated decoy pool gave a mean discrimination statistic (AUC) of 0.54 across the eight targets, against a published range for this docking method of approximately 0.6 to 0.85. A bootstrap analysis (2,000 resamples per target) found that only two of the eight targets' results were individually distinguishable from chance: one discriminated significantly better than chance, and one discriminated significantly worse. The remaining six targets' point estimates, though numerically spread across a wide range, each had confidence intervals overlapping the chance value and are not individually distinguishable from noise at this sample size. Of 28 pairwise comparisons between targets, 16 appeared nominally significant, but only four survived correction for multiple comparisons, all involving the single best-performing target. This is reported as the honest reliability ceiling of the benchmark at this decoy-set size, rather than treating the full point-estimate spread as established.

A supplementary analysis redocked the reaction-product, rather than intact, form of the ligand at the four targets where that form is the one genuinely observed in the crystal structure. One statistically robust result emerged: at SHV-1, the reaction-product form discriminated significantly worse than the intact drug (AUC 0.621 versus 0.233), consistent with that product's greater conformational freedom relative to the compact geometry the binding pocket was structured around. The remaining three comparisons were not statistically distinguishable in either direction.

### Covalent-Docking Feasibility Assessment

Because several targets form covalent bonds with the enzyme, a mechanism that is not explicitly represented by the standard docking protocol used elsewhere in this project, a genuine covalent-docking tool and a machine-learning rescoring alternative were evaluated for SHV-1, the one target with an unresolved discrimination problem. The covalent-docking tool was found to have no local, open-source implementation and was excluded on that basis before any comparison was attempted. The rescoring alternative was installed and validated against its own reference example, then applied to the target's existing docked poses; it did not improve discrimination and, if anything, moved the result in the opposite direction.

In the course of this investigation, SHV-1's true ligand-binding position was established, using a separately solved, structurally aligned homologous complex, since this target's own receptor structure is deposited without any bound ligand. This showed the docked pose to be 5.6 Å from its true crystallographic position, substantially further than every other validated target in this project (under 1 Å in each case), indicating that the underlying limitation is in pose generation rather than in scoring, consistent with rescoring's failure to correct it.

### SHV-1 Box Correction

The same alignment also revealed that the original docking box for this target, centered on the catalytic residue by the same convention used at every other target, had in fact been positioned approximately 13 Å from the ligand's true location, a discrepancy that had gone undetected specifically because this target's receptor lacks a bound ligand of its own. A corrected, ligand-position-centered box modestly improved the discrimination statistic (from 0.621 to 0.724), though the improvement itself was not statistically confirmed against the original. More importantly, pose accuracy barely changed under the corrected box, indicating that recentering the search location alone does not resolve the underlying limitation identified above. The remaining seven docking targets were independently audited for the same vulnerability and confirmed not to share it, since each has a genuine bound ligand within its own deposited structure.

## Stage 3: Comparative Mutation Tracking

Curated resistance-mutation matching requires agreement on gene identity, amino-acid position, and both wild-type and mutant residue. Requiring the wild-type residue to match correctly rejected an apparent hit at *pncA* that arose from an unrelated gene in a different species sharing the same symbol and position number by coincidence. A separate comparison identified a canonical fluoroquinolone-resistance mutation (*gyrA* S83Y) at the correct position with correct wild-type agreement, which nonetheless does not correspond to any allele in the curated resistance database at that position; this is reported as a gap in the reference database's curation rather than a pipeline error, and the filter was not loosened to accommodate it.

Extending the analysis to all six ESKAPE species produced curated resistance hits for three of seven strain comparisons and none for the remaining four. Each zero-hit result was confirmed to be the biologically correct outcome. n every case, the strain's known resistance mechanism involves the acquisition of an entire mobile genetic element rather than a point substitution in an existing gene. This falls outside the scope of the variant-matching approach, which is designed to detect specific amino-acid substitutions. The corresponding resistance determinants were independently confirmed by the gene-profiling stage. One positive finding in this extension, a regulatory mutation (*nalC*) in *P. aeruginosa*, was independently corroborated by an unrelated feature-detection method used in Stage 5, providing cross-method validation of the same result.

## Stage 4: Mutation-Impact Simulation

### Validation Safeguards

An early check identified that the structural-modeling tool's raw output is not fully compliant with the standard file format, and that a downstream format-conversion tool could silently produce an empty output file from this malformed input without returning an error, which would in turn allow the docking tool to report a spurious near-zero binding-affinity result rather than failing visibly. Explicit checks were added at each step of this handoff, verifying the modeling tool's output before conversion and independently verifying the converted file's content before docking, so that this failure mode surfaces immediately rather than producing a plausible but meaningless result.

### Structural and Numbering Verification

Simulated mutations were selected from database-curated or literature-reported resistance variants, with residue numbering independently verified against the relevant structure before use in every case rather than trusted from the source's stated position.

At PDC-1, a mutation's residue number differed between two conventions used in the same source literature (residue 219 under one scheme, 247 under another); the correct residue was identified by locating the literature's own flanking sequence motif directly within the structure's sequence, rather than trusting either label. At PBP2a, a directly solved structure of the clinically relevant double mutant was available and was used in place of computational modeling entirely, giving a 1.1 kcal/mol reduction in predicted binding affinity and providing the strongest evidence class obtained in this stage. At OXA-23, structures of the reported mutant existed but were excluded after verification showed they carried an additional substitution at the catalytic lysine already established as mechanistically central to this enzyme's chemistry, which would have confounded the comparison; computational modeling was used instead, extended to apply the full combination of substitutions reported in the literature as a single variant. At PBP5, the deposited structure was found to carry a natural polymorphism relative to the literature reference (a threonine rather than the expected methionine at the relevant position), confirmed by direct sequence alignment rather than assumed to be a numbering error; the nearest achievable substitution was used as an explicitly stated proxy, and a second component of the literature-reported genotype, a residue insertion, was left unmodeled because the modeling tool used is limited to substitutions, a limitation reported explicitly rather than approximated.

A fifth case, at P99/AmpC, initially returned an ambiguous result using the target's standard validated substrate. On review, the literature source for this mutation's resistance mechanism specifically concerns a different substrate (cefepime rather than cephalothin). Repeating the simulation with the mechanistically relevant substrate produced results consistent with the expected resistance direction; both results were retained on record, since the initial ambiguity itself demonstrates that the mutation's effect is substrate-specific rather than a general destabilization.

### Assay Infeasibility

Several of the leading predictive mutations identified by the Stage 5 classifiers, primarily in GyrA and ParC, could not be simulated in this stage. Investigation confirmed that no GyrA structure exists for this organism in the Protein Data Bank at adequate specificity, and that the one usable ParC structure available would cover only one of the six relevant mutations. This was established through direct investigation.

## Stage 5: Machine-Learning Resistance-Phenotype Classifiers

### Cross-Validation and Leakage Assessment

The baseline classifier's reported accuracy was checked for inflation by clonal (study-batch) leakage using grouped cross-validation, in addition to a standard random split. No clonal leakage was found; a modest study-batch effect (approximately one to one and a half percentage points) was identified and reported, and accuracy remained between 96% and 98% under every split evaluated.

Extending the analysis to a species and resistance class for which gene-presence features are reported in the literature to be weak predictors provided a direct test of the limitation. An initial analysis at moderate scale found no clear net improvement from adding regulatory point-mutation features to gene-presence features, within the noise of that sample size, though the relevant mutation features ranked among the most predictive by feature-importance analysis, and one was independently confirmed present in a resistant genome exhibiting the expected functional consequence. Scaling the analysis to 1,020 genomes, using a pool size verified directly against the source database rather than an assumed figure, strengthened this signal: one model showed a modest net improvement in accuracy (0.836 to 0.851) from adding the regulatory features, while a second model showed a small decline on the same data, a genuinely model-dependent result reported as such rather than resolved into one summary number.

### Extension Across ESKAPE Species

A single, species-parameterized training script was used to extend the classifier to the three remaining ESKAPE species rather than duplicating code per species. In the *E. faecium* extension, every one of the classifier's fifteen most predictive features corresponded to a known vancomycin-resistance gene-cluster member, provided a accurate single-mechanism validation. The *E. cloacae* extension returned with a negative result: none of the expected resistance-family markers appeared among the classifier's leading features. This was verified to be mechanistically expected rather than a modeling failure, since that gene family is present in over 94% of genomes in this species regardless of resistance phenotype, making presence or absence uninformative for a mechanism that is actually regulatory rather than acquisition-based. Species identity for every genome used in this extension was independently verified against its own taxonomic classification record before inclusion.

### Grouped Cross-Validation and Dominant-Clone Bias

Two species' grouped-split evaluations were flagged as potentially unreliable, due to one sequence type or source study accounting for a large share of the dataset. A general criterion was developed to determine whether the imbalance could be addressed by excluding the dominant group while retaining sufficient diversity for analysis, or whether the remaining data were too limited for a reliable assessment. The same criterion was applied across all four flagged cases.

In *A. baumannii*, a previously reported (1.000) classification accuracy was found, under a properly constructed held-out evaluation (389 genomes across 39 sequence types never seen in training) that excluded the dominant clone from the training set, to have been an artifact of an undersized, degenerate test fold in the original evaluation. True held-out accuracy is approximately 0.80 to 0.87, clearly above chance but far below the originally reported figure. In *E. faecium*, by contrast, the same reliability flag was found to have been triggered by the algorithm in the automated fold-construction check rather than a genuine diversity problem: the dominant sequence type accounted for only 12% of the data, below the 15% threshold the check itself uses to flag concern. A properly sized, 830-genome held-out evaluation confirmed the original accuracy, approximately 0.98 to 0.99. A related scarcity limitation in the same species' source-study grouping was traced to a genuine gap in the source database's own metadata coverage. Checking the full pool of over 9,000 *E. faecium* genomes in the source database, independent of resistance phenotype, confirmed that source-study metadata is generally well populated for this species, and that the scarcity is specific to genomes carrying both a usable resistance phenotype and that metadata simultaneously.

## Stage 6: CRISPR-Cas and Acquired-Resistance Co-occurrence

### Initial and Extended Cohorts

An initial small-sample comparison in *K. pneumoniae* was consistent with the literature's reported inverse relationship between a functional CRISPR-Cas system and acquired resistance-gene burden, reported with the explicit caveat that this held along one specific measure and not along total resistance-gene count, and that the sample size did not support a formal statistical test.

Extending this comparison across all six ESKAPE species at small sample size found that CRISPR-Cas presence varies enough to test a relationship in only three species; of those three, one supported the literature's reported relationship and two contradicted it. This mixed outcome, including the finding that the literature-associated CRISPR-Cas subtype does not appear outside the species in which the relationship was originally described.

### Population-Scale Reanalysis

Scaling the comparison in *P. aeruginosa* to 1,020 genomes reversed the small-sample result: acquired-resistance burden was significantly lower in CRISPR-Cas-positive genomes across three independent statistical tests (Mann-Whitney p = 2.9 × 10⁻¹²), consistent with the literature's reported relationship. The original small-sample contradiction is attributable to the trivial magnitude of a two-genome comparison rather than a real species-level pattern. Subtype analysis at this scale also overturned the earlier finding that only one species carries the literature-associated subtype: *P. aeruginosa* was found to carry substantial representation of it as well, though the burden-lowering effect itself does not depend on which subtype is present.

A parallel scale-up in *S. aureus* (400 genomes) confirmed that its very low CRISPR-Cas prevalence (0.75%) as a population-level characteristic; the resulting sample of positive genomes was too small to support a statistical test in either direction.

### Confounding by Clonal Population Structure

Population-scale reanalysis was extended to the remaining species . In *A. baumannii* (n=382), a naive population-wide comparison showed no relationship (p = 0.227). However, 44% of the sampled genomes belonged to a single, globally dominant clone that was uniform on the tested variable, entirely CRISPR-Cas-absent with high resistance-gene burden, contributing statistical dilution to the naive test. Excluding this clone revealed a strong, statistically significant relationship in the direction that contradicts the literature (Mann-Whitney p = 7.1 × 10⁻⁸; Fisher's exact odds ratio 5.55, p = 1.5 × 10⁻⁸, against resistance-gene burden).

In *E. faecium* (n=322, four CRISPR-Cas-positive genomes), a nominally significant result from one statistical test (p = 0.017) was not corroborated by companion tests on the same small sample (p = 0.13), and is unreliable rather than as a finding, internal inconsistency across tests on the same data is informative at very small sample sizes. Extension also identified the first non-Type-I CRISPR-Cas system in project. Results in *E. cloacae* (n=106) were explicitly lower confidence than the other two, since a pre-existing imbalance in the labeled dataset for this species (over 97% of usable genomes are resistant) makes the relevant statistical comparison degenerate regardless of CRISPR-Cas status.

When aggregated, the six-species analysis in this stage found relationships in three different directions across three testable species, with the specific direction determined by species-specific factors such as circulating clonal structure: a conclusion drawn directly from the population-scale data rather than assumed from the literature's single-species mechanism.

## Results Presentation

The results dashboard was verified programmatically across every stage, species, and target combination it presents, and a sample of its displayed values was independently verified. Two data-completeness limitations are noted for correct interpretation of the dashboard's output: one docking target predates this project's standard result-file format and is presented from its raw log output with a corresponding note.

## Summary

Across the six pipeline stages, a consistent verification framework was applied throughout the analysis. External identifiers, structural information, and reported results were validated against their primary sources or evaluated using independently constructed statistical checks before being incorporated into the workflow. This approach established data provenance, supported methodological traceability, and strengthened the reproducibility of the analyses across stages. Where the validation process identified discrepancies or limitations, these were incorporated into the documented methodology and used to refine the relevant analyses. All figures and quantitative results cited in this document are drawn directly from the results and logs described in  `README.md` and under `results/`.
